A second, looser band now sits beside the strict identification pass rather than replacing it: `tentative_identifications` runs after `identify_clusters` completes and scores only fragments — clusters under `TENTATIVE_FLOOR_SECONDS` (5.0 s, the same value as enroll's prompt floor, pinned by a test in `meethook-enroll` because that constant is `pub(crate)` there) — against names the strict pass already strictly awarded elsewhere in this session, through the same unthresholded per-person ranking the strict pass uses, cut at `TENTATIVE_DISTANCE` (0.42, compared strictly-below exactly the way `best_match` compares `IDENTIFY_DISTANCE`, so the calibration report and the shipping pass cannot differ by one `<`). The strict pass itself is untouched and byte-identical; the band is additive and vacuous when nobody was strictly identified in-session, which is the normal state of a short recording. A guess renders as `Name?` through `Attribution::label()`: the marker rides the label string into `Turn.speaker`, so the json/md/VTT renderers see an ordinary string (`<v Ivan?>` voice spans carry it harmlessly), `transcript.json`'s on-disk shape does not move, and no renderer changes — decision-010's contract holds. `confidence()` carries the real similarity and `is_named()` is false: the number is true, and the open-question bit is what keeps `enroll` surfacing these voices rather than reading a guess as a settled answer.

This revises decision-004's ruling against a middle tier between match and no-match. Its stated reason was that no interface existed capable of resolving ambiguity; that condition no longer holds in the narrow form the band needs, because the guess is visible the moment it lands — a question mark a reader sees — and enrollment treats it as an open question rather than an answer. The reject-rather-than-guess bias survives where it matters: long voices keep their honest behaviour of staying unnamed until a person says otherwise, and the tool never guesses somebody who was not in the meeting. Two scopes are what make a looser cut admissible at all. Tail-only: a wrong guess costs one visibly-marked line rather than one person's words filed unmarked under another's name, which is the mistake `IDENTIFY_DISTANCE` prices itself against. In-session pool only: the guess space is the meeting's own confirmed cast — the finished definite map's image — and that restriction, not the width of the cut, is what makes the width safe.

The value is measured, not derived from `IDENTIFY_DISTANCE`. Over the two real multi-speaker sessions on file — `20260818-132033` (181 clusters over 17.1 min of speech, seven people, 145 of the clusters under the floor) and `20260818-143027` (21 clusters, 14 under the floor) — the heard-at-once negatives sit at minimum distance **0.207** and **0.509** respectively, and the nearest non-overlapping partner per fragment at minimum **0.231** / median **0.458**. The negative minimum bounds trust in the veto rather than the cut directly: production refuses exactly those pairs whatever the cut is, and a future change that weakens the veto must re-price the constant against 0.207. The upper bound is the ear-confirmed confusable pair the `IDENTIFY_DISTANCE` documentation records at **0.429**: a cut at or past it admits the geometry that misfiles one real person under another's name even in the marked form. The lower bound is the strict cut itself, below which the band is vacuous. The admissible window is therefore `(0.40, 0.429)` and the value is **0.42**, inside it and flagged for re-pricing once positives — a fragment and the cluster of the person it really is — can be labelled by ear, which neither `speaker_clusters.json` nor an unnamed transcript can supply. The populations and the rejected alternatives live in the constant's own documentation, per the discipline `IDENTIFY_DISTANCE` sets.

The veto runs with its full force against the union of definite and tentative holders, over the whole set as the award proceeds rather than against the winner alone — the non-transitivity case decision-004 documents — and a vetoed name loses only that slot: the fragment may still take its next pooled name if it clears the cut, which is the next entry of the ranked list, not a runner-up fallback on the same person's second reference. Fragments walk in ascending cluster id and names in descending similarity then ascending name, so a rerun with nothing changed moves no fragment between names. Standing denials suppress the guess they name, resolved by bit-exact embedding equality in one shared place called from both `transcribe` and `enroll`, so a preview and a write cannot resolve the same refusal to different rows.

## Considered options

- Keeping decision-004's single cut — honest, but leaves every sub-floor fragment a bare `Unknown N` even when the session has already confirmed who was in the room; the tail's cost model (one marked line) makes the looser cut cheap enough to be worth asking.
- Guessing against the whole enrolled database — would let the tool guess somebody who was not in the meeting; the in-session pool is what makes the width safe.
- Carrying the mark as a structured field in `transcript.json` — moves the on-disk shape decision-010 guards; the string carries it instead.
- Reusing `IDENTIFY_DISTANCE`'s value for the band — vacuous: a fragment the strict pass refused cannot clear a tighter cut against the same reference.
