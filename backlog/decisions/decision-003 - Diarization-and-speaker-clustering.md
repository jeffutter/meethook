Diarization decodes pyannote's `segmentation-3.0` ONNX model in 10-second, non-overlapping windows and embeds each turn with a WeSpeaker ResNet34 model, both run through the `ort` crate — chosen over NeMo's Sortformer (broken ONNX export) and TitaNet (less turnkey export than WeSpeaker) for a zero-Python-dependency pipeline. Turns are clustered by constrained agglomerative clustering with average linkage over cosine distance, stopped at a single measured threshold (`MERGE_DISTANCE = 0.45`), biased deliberately toward under-merging: a silent merge misattributes one person's words to another, while a missed merge only adds a visible extra "Unknown" speaker. Two supervision signals feed the clustering for free from segmentation's own output — a cannot-link constraint (turns segmentation heard in the same window under different local speakers can never merge) and a must-link seed (turns heard together under the same local speaker start pre-merged) — both free of any threshold or embedding cost.

Real-session measurement (a 43-minute, 7-person meeting) exposed that average linkage carries a mathematically exact size bias: for unit-length embeddings it equals `1 − shrinkage·(1 − centroid_distance)`, where shrinkage grows with a group's spread. A short 1–3 second fragment is charged "rent" on the noise of whatever cluster it's compared against, so genuinely short clusters never merge into the speaker they belong to no matter how close their centroid actually is — a session with a handful of real speakers produced 95 clusters. Neither obvious fix works: raising the threshold to absorb fragments would also merge two people whose real distance sits inside the same range, and switching wholesale to centroid linkage would merge a pair of real speakers the average-linkage constraint correctly kept apart. The actual fix is a second pass that offers every cluster under a 30-second floor to the nearest cluster above it, adopting only within a separately measured, tighter threshold (`ADOPTION_DISTANCE = 0.25`) derived from segmentation-labelled same/different-speaker populations — and explicitly allowed to decline, since a real participant who only ever produces short clusters must not be swept into someone else's identity.

Fragments the adoption pass declines to place stay visible as their own small clusters rather than being suppressed. This was a deliberate, user-ratified call: the merge step that assembles the final transcript falls back to attributing any unclaimed span to whichever turn is nearest in time, so a suppressed fragment doesn't disappear — it silently reattaches to a neighbor. A visible extra "Unknown" is a cheap, inspectable error; a silent reattachment is not. After the fix, the closest measurable gap between clustering output (dozens of small residual clusters) and a usable enrollment experience (roughly one question per real participant) was closed on the enrollment side with a talk-time floor on which clusters get offered to the user, rather than by further threshold tuning — three independently measured ceilings (the closest two real speakers sit 0.429 apart; must-link supervision was fully consumed; most residual clusters were single turns under two seconds) showed no threshold could close that gap on its own.

## Considered options

- Single linkage or complete linkage instead of average linkage — chain speakers together through one ambiguous turn, or shatter one speaker over a single bad clip.
- Raising `MERGE_DISTANCE` to absorb short fragments — the same-speaker and different-speaker populations measurably overlap in that range; it would merge real people.
- Switching to centroid (single-vector) linkage — would have merged an ear-confirmed distinct pair of speakers that average linkage correctly separated.
- Suppressing residual fragments instead of leaving them visible — rejected; the merge step's nearest-turn fallback means suppression is silent misattribution, not silence.
- Asking the user how many participants there are — rejected on principle; the tool should never require a headcount it can infer.
